## What I Understood

### Project Goal

- Build a Java application that takes a garden image (or video frame) as input and **counts the number of birds** present
- The system must distinguish birds from other objects — leaves, branches, shadows, background grass

### Why Bird Counting is Challenging

- Birds are often **small relative to the image size** — they can be just a few pixels across
- Birds can **overlap or be partially hidden** behind leaves or branches
- **Background complexity** in a garden — trees, grass, shadows — makes separation difficult
- **Variable lighting** — a sunny garden has bright spots and dark shadows that change contrast
- **Movement** in video frames means bird positions change rapidly between frames

### Processing Pipeline I Planned

**Step 1 — Image Acquisition**
- Load a JPG or PNG image of the garden as input
- Resize if needed to a manageable resolution

**Step 2 — Grayscale Conversion**
- Convert the colour (RGB) image to grayscale
- Formula: `Y = 0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B`
- This reduces three channels to one, making all further operations simpler and faster
- Birds typically have darker or distinctly different intensity from the background grass

**Step 3 — Noise Reduction**
- Apply a **Gaussian filter** to smooth the image and remove sensor noise
- This prevents random noise pixels from being misidentified as bird edges later
- Kernel size: typically 3×3 or 5×5 depending on image size and noise level

**Step 4 — Contrast Enhancement**
- Apply **histogram equalization** if the image is low-contrast
- Makes intensity differences more visible — important if the garden is in dim lighting

**Step 5 — Thresholding / Segmentation**
- Apply **Otsu's method** to automatically find the best threshold value
- Pixels above the threshold → foreground (potential birds); below → background
- For garden images with uneven lighting, **adaptive thresholding** might be needed

**Step 6 — Edge Detection**
- Apply **Sobel operator** to detect edges (sharp intensity changes) in the thresholded image
- Bird outlines appear as strong edges; flat areas of grass produce weak gradients
- This helps sharpen the boundary between detected bird regions and the background

**Step 7 — Morphological Operations (Optional)**
- **Dilation** → expands detected regions, helps connect broken edges
- **Erosion** → shrinks regions, removes small noise blobs
- **Opening/Closing** → combines erosion and dilation to clean up the binary image

**Step 8 — Connected Component Labeling (Counting)**
- Scan the binary image and label each connected group of foreground pixels as one object
- Each labeled region is a **potential bird**
- Filter regions by **minimum and maximum area** to remove noise blobs (too small) and trees/bushes (too large)
- The number of remaining labeled regions = **estimated bird count**

**Step 9 — Display Result**
- Highlight detected birds on the original image (draw bounding boxes or circles)
- Display the total bird count

### Challenges I Identified

- **False positives** — leaves, rocks, shadows can be incorrectly flagged as birds
- **Overlapping birds** — two birds close together may be counted as one region
- **Size variability** — birds close to the camera appear larger than birds far away
- Solving these completely would require more advanced techniques (machine learning), but basic image processing can get a reasonable estimate

## My Reflection

- Mapping the textbook concepts directly onto a real project finally made everything concrete — grayscale conversion, filtering, thresholding, and edge detection are no longer abstract operations, they are specific steps I need to implement
- Otsu's method feels like the right choice for automatic thresholding — I don't want to manually choose a threshold for every different garden image
- Sobel edge detection after Gaussian blur is a classic pipeline and I now understand exactly why each step is there — blur first to avoid detecting noise as edges, then Sobel to find real boundaries
- Connected component labeling is the key counting step — once each bird is an isolated labeled region, counting is just counting the labels
- The hardest practical challenge will be filtering out false positives — I'll need to experiment with size thresholds to find what works for typical garden bird images
